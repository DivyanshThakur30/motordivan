📝 Prepare first

Before you visit the car showroom

Most buying mistakes happen because people walk into a showroom without a plan. Spend an hour on these four things at home and you will save time, money and stress later.

💰

Fix your total budget

Count more than the price. Add insurance, fuel, servicing, parking and the EMI. A common rule of thumb is to keep your monthly car cost to a comfortable share of your income, so it never strains the household budget.

🎯

Shortlist 2 or 3 cars

Decide how you will use the car: city driving, highway trips or a family of five. Then compare two or three models, not twenty. Choose fuel type and transmission to match your daily running.

🏦

Check your loan options

💡 Smart buying

10 car buying tips every buyer should know

Research the market price

Compare the same model, year, variant and KM driven across several listings so you know what a fair price looks like.

Pick the right variant

A mid variant with the features you will really use often gives better value than the top variant with extras you will not.

Think about running cost

Check mileage, service cost, spare part availability and insurance premium for the model, not just the purchase price.

Prefer fewer owners

A first-owner car with a full service history is usually easier to trust than a car that has changed hands many times.

Always take a test drive

Drive on a rough road and in traffic. Listen for noises, check the clutch, brakes, steering and AC, and see if the car pulls to one side.

Check the service history

Ask for service records and invoices. Regular servicing at an authorised or reputed workshop is a good sign.

Verify the papers first

Check the RC, insurance and PUC before you talk about the price. A great deal is no good if the papers are not clear.

Look for certified cars

Cars with the InventoryDekho Certified badge are checked by professionals, which makes shortlisting faster.

Do not rush

Do not decide under pressure such as "last piece" or "offer ends today". A good car and a good dealer will still be there tomorrow.

Get everything in writing

Price, free accessories, warranty, service promises and delivery date should all be on paper, signed or stamped by the dealer.

🏬 At the showroom

Showroom buying guide: step by step

Follow these steps in order when you visit a used car showroom or a new car dealership. Each step protects you from a common problem.

  1. Call ahead and confirm the carConfirm the exact car, price and availability on the phone. Ask the dealer to keep the papers ready so your visit is not wasted.
  2. Inspect the car in daylightLook at the car in good light, not at night or in a dim showroom. Use the inspection checklist below and check every panel, tyre and light.
  3. Take a proper test driveDrive for at least 15 to 20 minutes on different roads. Try the brakes, steering, gearbox, AC and music system, and park the car once.
  4. Check the papersMatch the chassis and engine number on the RC with the car. See the paperwork checklist for what to collect.
  5. Ask for an itemised price in writingFor a new car, ask for the full on-road price breakup. For a used car, ask what is included: transfer charges, insurance, warranty and any service.
  6. Negotiate and compareUse what you found in the inspection and the market price to negotiate. Compare at least one more dealer before you commit.
  7. Finalise payment and paperworkPay through bank transfer, take a signed receipt and sign the transfer forms only after you have checked every detail. Check insurance offers for the car.
  8. Check the car at deliveryOn the day of delivery, check the car again. Confirm both keys, the manual, tool kit, spare wheel, and that every promised item is delivered.

🔍 Used car check

Used car inspection checklist

Print this or keep it on your phone when you go to see a second hand car. If you are not confident, take a trusted mechanic with you.

🚘 Exterior and body

  • Panel gaps are even on all sides
  • Paint colour and shine match on every panel
  • No rust on the doors, boot, wheel arches or under the car
  • Windscreen and glass have no cracks, and all lights work
  • Tyres have even wear and good tread, and the spare is present

🔧 Engine and underbody

  • Engine starts easily and idles smoothly with no strange noise
  • No oil, coolant or fluid leaks under the car
  • Engine oil and coolant look clean and are at the right level
  • No excess smoke from the exhaust
  • Battery, belts and hoses look healthy

🪑 Interior and electricals

  • Seat wear matches the KM shown on the odometer
  • AC cools well, and the blower and all vents work
  • Power windows, central locking, mirrors and infotainment work
  • No musty smell or damp carpets, which can be a sign of water damage
  • All warning lights switch off after the engine starts

🛣 Test drive

  • Car goes straight and does not pull to one side
  • Brakes are firm with no vibration or noise
  • Clutch and gears are smooth, with no jerks
  • Suspension feels stable on bumps, with no knocking sounds
  • Steering is light and returns to centre smoothly

📄 Documents

Paperwork checklist for buying a used car

Clean paperwork is as important as a clean engine. Ask for these documents and check each one before you pay the full amount.

Documents to collect when buying a used car in India
DocumentWhat to check
Registration Certificate (RC)Original RC. Chassis and engine numbers must match the car, and the seller's name must match the RC.
Insurance policyValid policy. Ask about the no claim bonus and get the policy transferred to your name.
PUC certificateValid pollution under control certificate.
Service recordsService book and invoices, to confirm regular maintenance and the real KM driven.
Form 29 and Form 30Transfer of ownership forms, signed by the seller. Keep copies and apply for transfer promptly.
Form 35 and bank NOCNeeded if the car was under a loan. Confirms the loan is closed and the bank's hypothecation is removed from the RC.
RTO NOCMay be needed if the car was registered in a different state or RTO. Confirm with your local RTO.
Sale receiptSigned receipt with car details, price, payment mode and date.
Your ID and address proofThe buyer needs these for the ownership transfer. Keep copies ready.

🧾 New car pricing

On-road price explained

The price on the advertisement is rarely the price you pay. Ask the showroom for an itemised quote so you can see where every rupee goes.

Parts of a new car on-road price in India
ComponentWhat it means
Ex-showroom priceThe price of the car before taxes and charges for registration and insurance.
Road tax and registrationCharged by the state. The amount depends on your state, the car price and the fuel type.
InsuranceFirst-year comprehensive insurance. You can compare it with outside offers before you accept the dealer's quote.
Handling or logistics chargesDealer or manufacturer charges. Ask what exactly they cover.
TCSTax collected at source on higher priced cars, as per current rules. Confirm the latest rate and limit with the dealer.
Accessories and extrasOptional add-ons such as seat covers, floor mats, coatings and extended warranty. You can say no to anything you do not need.

🤝 How to negotiate

  • Know the market price before you walk in
  • Be polite and calm, and take your time
  • Point out real issues found in inspection, such as tyres, scratches or pending service
  • Ask for extras instead of only a lower price: free service, accessories, a warranty or insurance help
  • Negotiate the total price, not only the monthly EMI
  • Show your coupon code to the dealer to claim your InventoryDekho discount
  • Be ready to walk away. It often gives you the best offer

🚩 Red flags to avoid

  • A price far below the market with no clear reason
  • The seller refuses an inspection or a test drive
  • RC is missing, not in the seller's name, or numbers do not match
  • Pressure to pay in cash or to pay the full amount quickly
  • Odometer reading that does not match the car's wear
  • Signs of flood damage, such as a damp smell or silt under the carpets
  • Pending loan or challans that the seller does not want to discuss

Frequently Asked Questions about buying a car

What should I check before buying a used car?

Check the car's papers first (RC, insurance, PUC and service history), then inspect the exterior, engine, interior and electricals, and always take a test drive. Look for uneven panel gaps, fresh paint on only some panels, oil leaks, worn tyres and a musty smell inside. If possible, get the car checked by a trusted mechanic before you pay.

Which documents are needed to buy a used car in India?

You should receive the original Registration Certificate (RC), a valid insurance policy, a valid PUC certificate, service records, and signed transfer forms (Form 29 and Form 30). If the car was under a loan, ask for Form 35 and the bank's NOC or loan closure letter. If the car was registered in another state, an NOC from that RTO may also be needed. Keep a copy of the sale receipt showing the amount paid.

How do I negotiate the price at a car showroom?

Research the market price of the same model, year and KM driven first. Ask for an itemised price in writing, point out genuine issues you found during inspection, compare quotes from two or three dealers, and be ready to walk away politely. Negotiate the total price you pay, not just the monthly EMI.

What is on-road price?

On-road price is the total you actually pay to drive the car out of the showroom. For a new car it includes the ex-showroom price, road tax and registration, first-year insurance, handling charges and any extras such as accessories or an extended warranty. Always ask the dealer for an itemised on-road price quote.

How soon should I transfer ownership after buying a used car?

The buyer should apply for transfer of ownership at the RTO within 30 days of purchase, and the seller must also report the sale. Also get the insurance policy transferred to your name soon after purchase, and ask your insurer about their time limit. Delays can leave you responsible for challans or accidents that happen in the previous owner's name.

Is it safe to pay in cash for a used car?

It is better to pay by bank transfer or cheque so that there is a clear record, and to take a signed receipt with the car details, price and date. Avoid paying large amounts in cash, and do not hand over the full payment before the papers are checked and the transfer forms are signed.
